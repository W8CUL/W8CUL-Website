Balloon Project

The W8CUL High-Altitude Balloon (HAB) Project combines amateur radio, atmospheric science, and hands-on engineering to launch payloads to the edge of space.

Our balloons reach altitudes of 100,000+ feet, capturing stunning imagery and valuable scientific data while providing real-world experience in radio tracking, payload design, and recovery operations.

Project Goals

Education

Provide hands-on experience in radio communications, electronics, and atmospheric science

Radio Skills

Practice APRS tracking, direction finding, and real-time telemetry decoding

Research

Conduct atmospheric measurements and test experimental payloads

Outreach

Engage the WVU community and inspire interest in STEM and amateur radio

Technical Specifications

Balloon System

  • Balloon: Kaymont KCI 1200g latex
  • Lift Gas: Hydrogen or Helium
  • Neck Lift: ~1800g
  • Ascent Rate: ~5 m/s target
  • Burst Altitude: 90,000 - 110,000 ft
  • Parachute: 48" rip-stop nylon

Tracking System

  • Primary: APRS on 144.390 MHz
  • Tracker: W8CUL TNC Kit + Baofeng UV-5R
  • Backup: Spot Trace satellite tracker
  • Telemetry: GPS, altitude, temperature, voltage
  • TX Power: 5W
  • Antenna: Quarter-wave ground plane

Imaging Payload

  • Camera: GoPro Hero 11 Black
  • Resolution: 5.3K @ 60fps
  • Interval Photos: 0.5 second timelapse
  • Storage: 256GB microSD
  • Battery: Extended cold-weather pack

Payload Box

  • Material: Expanded polystyrene (EPS)
  • Dimensions: 8" x 8" x 10"
  • Weight: ~800g total payload
  • Insulation: -60°C rated
  • Recovery: Bright orange exterior, reflective tape

Live APRS Tracking

During active launches, track our balloon in real-time on aprs.fi using callsign W8CUL-11.

Get Involved

Build Team

Help design and assemble payload systems, trackers, and recovery equipment. Great for hands-on learners!

  • Soldering and electronics assembly
  • 3D printing and fabrication
  • Payload integration and testing

Tracking Team

Operate ground stations, decode APRS packets, and help with direction finding during recovery.

  • APRS setup and operation
  • Mobile tracking during flights
  • Antenna and radio operations

Recovery Team

Chase the balloon and retrieve payloads from landing sites across West Virginia and beyond.

  • Mobile chase operations
  • Navigation and terrain traversal
  • Recovery documentation

Science Team

Design experiments, analyze data, and contribute to research using the platform.

  • Atmospheric sensor design
  • Data analysis and visualization
  • Research collaboration
