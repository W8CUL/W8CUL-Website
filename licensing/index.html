Licensing

What is Amateur Radio?

Amateur (ham) radio is a popular hobby and service that brings people, electronics, and communication together.

Ham radio operators use various types of equipment to communicate across town, around the world, and even into space - all without the Internet or cell phones. When disasters knock out other communication channels, ham radio operators can provide critical emergency communications.

Getting your amateur radio license opens the door to:

  • Making contacts with people around the world
  • Building and experimenting with radio equipment
  • Participating in emergency communications
  • Competing in radio contests
  • Satellite and space communications
  • Technical experimentation and learning

License Classes Compared

Most Popular Starting Point

Technician

Entry-level license

Privileges

  • All amateur privileges above 30 MHz
  • Local repeater access (VHF/UHF)
  • Satellite communications
  • Limited HF privileges (10, 15, 40, 80m)

Band Access

VHF/UHF (2m, 70cm) + portions of 10, 15, 40, 80 meters

Exam Details

35 multiple choice questions, 26 correct to pass (74%)

Study for Technician

General

Intermediate license

Privileges

  • All Technician privileges
  • Most HF band privileges
  • Worldwide communication capability
  • DX (long-distance) contacts

Band Access

Voice privileges on 160, 80, 40, 20, 17, 15, 12, 10 meters

Exam Details

35 multiple choice questions, 26 correct to pass (74%). Requires Technician license.

Study for General

Amateur Extra

Highest license class

Privileges

  • All amateur radio privileges
  • Exclusive Extra-class band segments
  • Can volunteer as VE examiner
  • Vanity callsign eligibility (1x2, 2x1)

Band Access

Full privileges on all amateur bands, including exclusive Extra portions

Exam Details

50 multiple choice questions, 37 correct to pass (74%). Requires General license.

Taking Your Exam

What to Bring

  • XX exam fee (confirm current amount; cash preferred)
  • Two forms of ID (one must be a photo ID)
  • FRN (FCC Registration Number) - Required! Register here
  • Calculator (no memory functions)
  • Pencils and scratch paper (usually provided)
  • Copy of your current license (if upgrading)

Before the Exam

  1. Get your FRN from the FCC CORES system - this is mandatory!
  2. Study using the resources above until you're consistently passing practice exams
  3. Attend a Tech in A Day (TIAD) event or contact the club for help
  4. Check our events page for the next exam date

On Exam Day

Arrive 10-15 minutes early. The exam typically takes 30-60 minutes. If you pass, you can immediately take the next level exam at no additional cost!

Pro Tip: If you pass your Technician exam, consider taking the General exam the same day while the material is fresh. Many people upgrade on the same day!
